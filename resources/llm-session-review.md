# The LLM Session Review Checklist: Verified Pitfalls and Anti-Patterns for Engineers Building on the API

## TL;DR
- **The single highest-leverage habit is treating context as a scarce, degrading resource, not a free dumping ground.** Verified research — Chroma's "Context Rot" (Hong, Troynikov & Huber, July 2025); Liu et al. "Lost in the Middle" (TACL 2024); and Laban, Hayashi, Zhou & Neville "LLMs Get Lost in Multi-Turn Conversation" (2025, an ICLR 2026 outstanding paper) — shows that model accuracy degrades as input grows (even far below the context-window limit) and that models attend poorly to the middle of long contexts. Most "the model got dumber" complaints are context-management failures, not model failures.
- **The second highest-leverage habit is protecting your prompt-cache prefix.** A single dynamic token (timestamp, request ID, reordered tool list) placed early in a prompt invalidates the entire cached prefix, silently multiplying cost and latency. Per Anthropic's official docs, cache reads cost **10% of base input token price** and cache writes cost **25% more than base input** (5-minute TTL).
- **Use this as a graded review checklist**, organized by performance dimension (Context, Cost/Tokens, Latency, Caching, Tool Use/Agents, Prompt Structure, Reliability, Sampling, RAG, Coding Agents). Each item is flagged **OBVIOUS**, **SUBTLE**, or **RARELY-CONSIDERED**, with the concrete thing to check and its evidence basis.

## Key Findings
1. **More context is not better.** Performance degrades continuously with input length, beginning well before the window is full. This is empirically established across 18 frontier models (Chroma).
2. **Position matters as much as presence.** Information in the middle of a long context is attended to worst; the beginning and end are strongest ("lost in the middle").
3. **Multi-turn drift is real and large.** LLMs lose an average of 39% performance when a task is revealed across conversational turns versus a single prompt; once they take a wrong turn early, they do not recover.
4. **Caching is structural, not incidental.** The cache prefix is built in the fixed order tools → system → messages, hashed byte-for-byte; any upstream change invalidates everything downstream.
5. **Tool sprawl degrades reliability.** Past a threshold, adding tools reduces an agent's ability to use any of them correctly.
6. **Reasoning/thinking tokens are a budget item, not a free quality boost.** They add latency and cost (billed as output tokens) and help only on genuinely hard reasoning tasks.

## Details

### A. Context Management
- **A1. Over-stuffing the context window** — **OBVIOUS but under-acted-on.** Adding more documents/history than the task needs adds tokens without signal and actively degrades output. *Check:* For each block in context, ask "would removing this change the output?" If no, cut it. *Evidence:* Chroma "Context Rot" — performance degrades non-uniformly with input length even on simple tasks across 18 models; Anthropic "Effective context engineering for AI agents" — the discipline is finding "the smallest set of high-signal tokens that maximize the likelihood of your desired outcome."
- **A2. Context rot / degradation below the window limit** — **RARELY-CONSIDERED.** A 1M-token window still degrades at 50K tokens; the problem is noise accumulation, not capacity. *Check:* Track input-token count per turn as a health metric; degradation is gradual and silent (the model still answers, just worse). *Evidence:* Chroma "Context Rot" — abstract states models are "presumed to process context uniformly... However, in practice, this assumption does not hold... performance varies significantly as input length changes, even on simple tasks." Tested across 18 models including Claude Opus 4/Sonnet 4, GPT-4.1/o3, Gemini 2.5, and Qwen3.
- **A3. Lost in the middle** — **SUBTLE.** Accuracy is highest when key info is at the start or end of context, and drops significantly in the middle, even for explicitly long-context models. *Check:* Place the most important instructions/documents at the beginning or end, not buried mid-context; in RAG, don't bury the best chunk in the middle of many. *Evidence:* Liu et al., "Lost in the Middle: How Language Models Use Long Contexts," TACL 2024 (doi:10.1162/tacl_a_00638).
- **A4. Accumulating irrelevant tool outputs / history bloat** — **SUBTLE.** In agent loops, tool results, reasoning, and messages pile up; eventually you pay for context that no longer helps and degrade every subsequent output. *Check:* Implement tool-result clearing, compaction, or summarization at checkpoints. *Evidence:* Anthropic Claude Cookbook "Context engineering: memory, compaction, and tool clearing"; LangChain "Context Engineering" (write / select / compress / isolate).
- **A5. Multi-turn drift / context poisoning** — **RARELY-CONSIDERED.** An early wrong assumption compounds and the model does not self-correct; contaminated context propagates as if it were truth. *Check:* Consolidate/summarize requirements before generation; add validation checkpoints; when possible, re-state the full consolidated task in one turn. *Evidence:* Laban, Hayashi, Zhou & Neville, "LLMs Get Lost in Multi-Turn Conversation" (arXiv 2505.06120; ICLR 2026 outstanding paper) — "an average drop of 39% across six generation tasks" over "200,000+ simulated conversations," across 15 models from eight providers (incl. GPT-4.1, Claude 3.7 Sonnet, Gemini 2.5 Pro, DeepSeek-R1, Llama 4-Scout); degradation decomposes into "a minor loss in aptitude and a significant increase in unreliability... when LLMs take a wrong turn in a conversation, they get lost and do not recover." Also Elastic "Context poisoning in LLMs."
- **A6. Distractor sensitivity** — **SUBTLE.** Related-but-irrelevant content ("distractors") hurts more than neutral filler, and the harm grows with input length. Notably, model families differ: Claude models tend to abstain under ambiguity while GPT models more often hallucinate confidently. *Check:* Filter retrieved context for near-duplicates and topically-adjacent noise. *Evidence:* Chroma "Context Rot" — "Claude models consistently exhibit the lowest hallucination rates... tend to abstain when uncertain... In contrast, GPT models show the highest rates of hallucination, often generating confident but incorrect responses when distractors are present."

### B. Token / Cost Efficiency
- **B1. Re-sending large unchanging documents instead of caching** — **OBVIOUS.** *Check:* Stable documents/system content should sit in a cached prefix, not be re-paid every turn. *Evidence:* Anthropic prompt caching docs.
- **B2. Verbose/token-inefficient formatting** — **SUBTLE.** Verbose JSON with repeated keys wastes input tokens versus compact representations for uniform tabular data. *Check:* For large uniform arrays sent to the model, consider compact formats; keep JSON for nested/irregular data and for model output (constrained decoding). *Evidence:* TOON benchmark repository and multiple practitioner comparisons report ~30–50% token reduction for flat/tabular data (Halodoc reported up to 15% overall cost savings in production); caveat — gains evaporate for nested/non-uniform data.
- **B3. System-prompt bloat** — **SUBTLE.** Long, brittle, hardcoded system prompts cost tokens every call and create maintenance fragility. *Check:* Aim for the "right altitude" — specific enough to guide, general enough to be robust. *Evidence:* Anthropic "Effective context engineering for AI agents."
- **B4. Output length dominates latency and cost** — **RARELY-CONSIDERED.** Output generation is far slower per token than input prefill, so trimming output length is the biggest speed lever. *Check:* Set max_tokens to the realistic expected length; instruct concise outputs. *Evidence:* practitioner latency analyses (the often-cited "≈100 input tokens ≈ 1 output token in processing time" is a practitioner estimate, not from a primary source — treat as directional).

### C. Latency / Speed
- **C1. Not streaming where it helps perceived latency** — **OBVIOUS.** *Check:* Enable streaming for user-facing flows; time-to-first-token (TTFT) drives perceived snappiness. *Evidence:* provider KPI guides (TTFT vs. TTLT).
- **C2. max_tokens set too high** — **SUBTLE.** A high max_tokens reservation increases time-to-last-token and, on some providers, counts against TPM rate limits even if unused. *Check:* Cap max_tokens close to expected output. *Evidence:* OpenAI rate-limit docs; Azure OpenAI TPM docs.
- **C3. Oversized prompts inflate TTFT** — **SUBTLE.** Prefill is quadratic in input length; more input tokens means slower first token. *Check:* Trim the prompt; split complex prompts where possible. *Evidence:* Glean input-token/TTFT testing.

### D. Caching (Claude-specific, with cross-provider analogs)
- **D1. Dynamic content early in the prompt breaks the cache** — **RARELY-CONSIDERED, high-impact.** A timestamp, request ID, session ID, or `datetime.now()` in the system prompt changes the hashed prefix every call, dropping cache hit rate to ~zero. *Check:* Move all volatile content after the cached prefix; never embed live timestamps early. *Evidence:* Anthropic prompt caching docs — "Cache hits require 100% identical prompt segments, including all text and images up to and including the block marked with cache control"; multiple practitioner write-ups.
- **D2. Non-deterministic tool ordering silently invalidates the cache** — **RARELY-CONSIDERED.** Tools assembled from a dict/set can shuffle order between runs; since tools render first in the prefix, this invalidates tools + system + messages. *Check:* Serialize tools in a fixed, sorted order; lock it in a unit test. *Evidence:* Anthropic caching docs; practitioner guides.
- **D3. Switching models mid-session** — **SUBTLE.** Caches are model-scoped; switching throws away the entire cache. *Check:* Keep the main loop on one model; spawn separate calls for cheaper sub-tasks rather than swapping the main loop's model. *Evidence:* Anthropic caching docs.
- **D4. Changing tools/thinking/images mid-prefix** — **SUBTLE.** Modifying tool definitions, toggling extended-thinking budget, or adding/removing images invalidates downstream cache. *Check:* Hold the scaffold stable; use a tool-search approach to append schemas on demand rather than mutating the tool array. *Evidence:* Anthropic caching docs.
- **D5. Cache mechanics to internalize.** Prefix order is tools → system → messages; up to 4 breakpoints per request; default 5-minute TTL (1-hour available at additional cost); ~20-block lookback. Pricing is exact: cache **reads = 10%** of base input price, cache **writes = 25% more** than base input (5-min TTL). *Check:* Place static content first, mark breakpoints after it, and monitor `cache_read_input_tokens` / `cache_creation_input_tokens` — if read stays at zero across requests sharing a prefix, run a prefix-diff audit. *Evidence:* Anthropic prompt caching docs and news post; AWS Bedrock docs.

### E. Tool Use & Agent Loops
- **E1. Too many tools** — **RARELY-CONSIDERED.** Past a threshold, more tools degrade selection accuracy; the model picks plausible-but-wrong tools silently, or freezes/hallucinates a nonexistent tool. *Check:* Route to specialist agents with small, coherent toolsets ("one agent, few tools"); use dynamic tool activation/search. *Evidence:* Multiple agent-framework analyses and an arXiv production-workflow guide recommend "one agent, one tool" where possible. (A widely-circulated practitioner claim that accuracy dropped from 43% to 2% when scaling 4→51 tools could **not** be confirmed in Berkeley Function Calling Leaderboard primary sources — treat as unverified; the peer-reviewed BFCLv4 analysis instead documents that adding semantically related distractor functions yields an average drop of 1–8 points.)
- **E2. Poor tool descriptions / ambiguous names** — **SUBTLE.** Overlapping descriptions (`send_notification` vs `push_alert`) cause silent misselection. *Check:* Crisp, differentiated descriptions; unambiguous parameter names (`user_id` not `user`). *Evidence:* Anthropic "Writing tools for agents."
- **E3. Unbounded tool outputs** — **SUBTLE.** Large tool responses balloon context. *Check:* Paginate/filter/truncate with sensible default parameters; Claude Code restricts tool responses to 25,000 tokens by default. *Evidence:* Anthropic "Writing tools for agents."
- **E4. Over-engineering with frameworks** — **SUBTLE.** Heavy abstractions obscure the underlying prompts and tokens and tempt unnecessary complexity. *Check:* Start with direct API calls and simple composable patterns; add complexity only when justified; if you use a framework, understand the underlying code. *Evidence:* Anthropic "Building effective agents" — "the most successful implementations use simple, composable patterns rather than complex frameworks."
- **E5. Speculative/wasted tool calls** — **SUBTLE.** *Check:* Encourage many small targeted searches over a single broad one; prompt-engineer error responses to be specific and actionable rather than opaque tracebacks. *Evidence:* Anthropic "Writing tools for agents."

### F. Prompt Structure (Claude-specific where noted)
- **F1. Instructions before long documents** — **SUBTLE, Claude-specific gain.** For long-document/data-rich inputs (20k+ tokens), place documents at the top and instructions after; ask Claude to quote relevant parts first to cut through noise. *Check:* Document-then-instruction ordering; XML-tagged documents with `<document_content>` and `<source>` subtags. *Evidence:* Anthropic prompting best practices ("Long context tips").
- **F2. Unstructured "blob" prompts** — **SUBTLE, Claude-specific gain.** Mixing instructions, context, and examples without delimiters causes the model to conflate them (e.g., copying an example instead of doing the task). *Check:* Use XML tags (`<instructions>`, `<context>`, `<document>`, `<example>`); be consistent and nest hierarchically. *Evidence:* Anthropic "Use XML tags to structure your prompts."
- **F3. Vague/underspecified instructions** — **OBVIOUS.** *Check:* Treat the model like a capable new hire with no shared context; apply the "colleague test" — if a person with minimal context would be confused, so will the model. *Evidence:* Anthropic prompting docs.

### G. Reliability & Error Handling
- **G1. No exponential backoff / retry storms** — **OBVIOUS.** Immediate retries cause thundering-herd loops; unsuccessful requests still count against rate limits. *Check:* Exponential backoff with jitter; respect `retry-after` / `retry-after-ms` headers; for throughput workloads add a proactive delay near the reciprocal of your RPM. *Evidence:* OpenAI rate-limit docs and cookbook ("How to handle rate limits").
- **G2. Not using structured outputs** — **SUBTLE.** Free-text or bare JSON-mode parsing is brittle. JSON mode guarantees valid JSON but not schema adherence; strict Structured Outputs enforces the schema (gpt-4o-2024-08-06 scored 100% on OpenAI's schema-following eval vs. <40% for gpt-4-0613). *Check:* Use strict structured outputs / tool-schema enforcement; require `additionalProperties: false`; model optional fields as union-with-null; handle refusals as first-class errors. *Evidence:* OpenAI "Introducing Structured Outputs in the API."
- **G3. Not evaluating in multi-turn settings** — **RARELY-CONSIDERED.** Single-turn benchmarks overstate real-world performance. *Check:* Test agents on multi-turn, underspecified flows, not just fully-specified one-shot prompts. *Evidence:* Laban et al., "LLMs Get Lost in Multi-Turn Conversation."
- **G4. Silent failures** — **SUBTLE.** A confident, well-formatted, wrong answer passes surface review. *Check:* Log and audit tool selection and intermediate steps; add verification checkpoints. *Evidence:* Laban et al.; practitioner agent post-mortems.

### H. Sampling / Config
- **H1. Tuning temperature and top_p simultaneously** — **SUBTLE.** Both OpenAI and Anthropic recommend adjusting one, not both; compound effects are unpredictable. *Check:* Pick one knob per use case. *Evidence:* OpenAI and Anthropic docs; Prompt Engineering Guide.
- **H2. Wrong temperature for the task** — **OBVIOUS.** *Check:* ~0 for extraction/classification/code; higher (0.7–1.2) for creative work. Store sampling params as per-endpoint config, not one global default. *Evidence:* practitioner sampling references; promptingguide.ai.
- **H3. Expecting determinism** — **RARELY-CONSIDERED.** temperature=0 is near-deterministic, not guaranteed (batching + floating-point non-associativity). Anthropic exposes no stable seed parameter as of early 2026; OpenAI's seed is best-effort and breaks when `system_fingerprint` changes. *Check:* Promise stability "within X% of cases," not exact reproducibility; add a rule layer if true determinism is required. *Evidence:* provider docs; sampling references.
- **H4. Misusing extended thinking** — **RARELY-CONSIDERED.** Reasoning tokens bill as output tokens and add latency (seconds, not milliseconds); they help math/logic/code design and debugging but not factual lookup or formatting. Thinking tokens from prior turns also accumulate as input in multi-turn flows. *Check:* Enable only for genuinely hard reasoning routes; start at the minimum budget and increase incrementally (diminishing returns above ~32k); use batch processing for budgets above 32k to avoid timeouts. On Claude Opus 4.6 / Sonnet 4.6, prefer `type: "adaptive"`. *Evidence:* Anthropic extended thinking docs; multiple cost analyses (empirical 3–10x+ token multiplier when reasoning is enabled).

### I. RAG-Specific
- **I1. Poor chunking** — **SUBTLE.** Chunks that split a key fact or span multiple topics can't be recovered by any reranker. *Check:* Start ~400–512 tokens with recursive splitting and 10–20% overlap; treat chunk size as a tunable parameter per workload (smaller for factual queries, larger for analytical). *Evidence:* practitioner RAG guides; Weaviate chunking guidance reports the wrong strategy can create up to a 9% recall gap.
- **I2. No reranking** — **SUBTLE.** Bi-encoder retrieval trades fine-grained relevance; cross-encoder reranking recovers it but adds latency (one controlled study saw ~9.2x latency, 0.22s→2.02s). *Check:* Add reranking only when recall/precision gains beat the added latency. *Evidence:* RAG best-practice guides.
- **I3. Contextual retrieval** — **Claude-specific gain.** Anthropic's "Contextual Retrieval" reports Reranked Contextual Embeddings + Contextual BM25 reduced top-20-chunk retrieval failure rate by **67% (5.7% → 1.9%)** (Contextual Embeddings alone: 35%, 5.7%→3.7%; +BM25: 49%, 5.7%→2.9%), and prompt caching cuts the per-chunk LLM preprocessing cost by up to 90% while reducing latency >2x. *Check:* Consider contextual retrieval for ambiguous queries. *Evidence:* Anthropic "Contextual Retrieval in AI Systems."

### J. Coding Agent Sessions (Claude Code specifics)
- **J1. Not clearing/compacting context** — **OBVIOUS in the Claude Code community.** Context degradation is the primary failure mode. *Check:* Use `/clear` aggressively between unrelated tasks; maintain a CLAUDE.md; rely on compaction. *Evidence:* Anthropic "Best practices for Claude Code"; "Effective harnesses for long-running agents."
- **J2. Skipping planning** — **SUBTLE.** Per Anthropic's "How Anthropic teams use Claude Code," its RL Engineering team finds the autonomous first-attempt approach "succeeds on the first attempt about one-third of the time," and its Data Science team treats it "like a slot machine" — saving state, letting Claude run ~30 minutes, then accepting or restarting. Planning before implementation is non-negotiable for production code. *Check:* Use plan mode; write detailed specs first; commit early and often for rollback. *Evidence:* Anthropic "How Anthropic teams use Claude Code"; DataCamp synthesis.
- **J3. Cross-context-window memory loss** — **RARELY-CONSIDERED.** Each new session starts with no memory of the previous one; long tasks need bridging artifacts. *Check:* Use a claude-progress.txt + git history + an "initializer agent" pattern that sets up the environment on the first run. *Evidence:* Anthropic "Effective harnesses for long-running agents."
- **J4. Stale harness assumptions** — **RARELY-CONSIDERED.** Harness workarounds become dead weight as models improve — e.g., context resets added to combat Claude Sonnet 4.5's premature wrap-up ("context anxiety") were unnecessary on Opus 4.5. *Check:* Periodically re-test harness hacks against newer models and remove dead weight. *Evidence:* Anthropic "Scaling Managed Agents."

## Recommendations
**Stage 1 — Instrument first (this week).** Log per-turn input-token count, `cache_read_input_tokens` / `cache_creation_input_tokens`, output tokens, TTFT, and tool-selection decisions. You cannot manage context rot or cache hit rate you don't measure. *Threshold to act:* if cache_read stays at zero across requests that share a prefix, run a prefix-diff audit immediately and look for an early dynamic token or reordered tools.

**Stage 2 — Fix the cache prefix and context hygiene (this sprint).** Move all dynamic content after the cached prefix; sort tools deterministically and unit-test the order; pin one model per loop. Implement compaction / tool-result clearing for any agent loop that runs more than a few turns. *Threshold:* if a session routinely exceeds ~50% of the window or shows quality dips late in long sessions, add compaction/summarization.

**Stage 3 — Structure prompts and outputs (this sprint).** Adopt XML-tagged, document-before-instruction structure for Claude; switch to strict structured outputs and treat refusals as errors; set per-task sampling configs (one of temperature/top_p); cap max_tokens to expected length.

**Stage 4 — Harden agents (next).** Audit toolset size and description quality; route to specialist sub-agents; add backoff-with-jitter and `retry-after` handling; add multi-turn evals and verification checkpoints. *Threshold:* if an agent carries more than ~10–15 tools or you observe silent misselection, split into specialist agents or add tool-search/dynamic activation.

**Stage 5 — Tune reasoning and RAG (ongoing).** Enable extended thinking only on hard reasoning routes and start at minimum budget; tune chunking and add reranking only where evals show gains beat the latency cost; consider contextual retrieval for ambiguous-query corpora.

## Caveats
- **Strength of evidence varies.** "Lost in the Middle" (peer-reviewed TACL), the Chroma 18-model report, the Anthropic engineering/docs material, and the Laban et al. multi-turn paper (ICLR 2026 outstanding paper) are strong primary sources. Caching mechanics and pricing come from official Anthropic/OpenAI/AWS docs. Token-format claims (TOON), the exact tool-count threshold, the "≈100 input tokens ≈ 1 output token" ratio, and the circulated "43%→2%" BFCL figure come from practitioner sources or could not be confirmed in primary sources — validate on your own workload and treat the dramatic tool-count figure as unverified.
- **Models improve.** Some pitfalls (context anxiety, specific tool-count breakpoints, thinking-budget sweet spots) are model-version-specific and shift across releases — re-test against current models.
- **One contrarian note:** at least one practitioner reanalysis argues the 39% multi-turn drop is partly a lab artifact of contradictory sharded prompts. The direction (multi-turn underspecification hurts, and early errors compound) is robust across sources; the exact magnitude may not generalize to every workload.
- **Scope.** This checklist optimizes performance, cost, and reliability; it is not a safety or security review. Prompt injection, jailbreaks (e.g., multi-turn "Echo Chamber" context-poisoning attacks), and data exfiltration are real but out of scope here.
